Foragers of the Bay

For those with trained eyes and hands, food is available all around.
Foraging unsettles the line between what counts as food and what does not.
You will not find miner’s lettuce, wild mustard pickles, or oddly shaped lemons in the supermarket, yet they are everywhere.
Food changes with seasons. Mussels are gathered in months with an “r”. Seaweed shifts with cold currents. Fennel goes to seed along roadsides in the heat.
Magnolia trees bloom from winter into spring, leaving behind a blanket of pink petals that disappears back into the ground. Nothing stays fixed.
Nature moves in cycles of arrival and disappearance. Foraging anchors us right into the present time and space.
This project follows foragers in the Bay Area building a relationship with the local ecosystem through practice.
They learn edible species, how to harvest them, and how to work with what the land has to offer.
In tidepools in Mendocino, they pull purple sea urchins from rocks, making space for kelp forests to return.
They gather wild mustard, fennel fronds, and nasturtium seeds, packing them into jars with brine until they ferment and shift.
They learn to slaughter sheep, and how to use every part of the animal.
Photographing them is witnessing an emerging and ancient way of belonging and reciprocity.
A way of learning this place my immersing in the stories and flavors of the land.
